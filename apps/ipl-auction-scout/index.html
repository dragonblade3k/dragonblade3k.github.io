Batting and bowling: runs a player adds per match compared with an average player in the same phase and competition, against the same strength of opponent, translated to IPL level. Chance bought = chance he is in the auction (released or unsigned, registers, makes BCCI's final list) × chance a team buys him once he's in. This is an early view: last December, more than half the uncapped buys did their best work in the Syed Mushtaq Ali Trophy or state leagues in the weeks before the auction, so the board will move a lot once this season's games are in. Likely price is rough: it misses by 30 to 50 lakh on a typical player and can't see bidding wars.

Did it see India players coming?

13 of 16 batters who debuted for India since 2023 were in our top 10% the day before debut (Jaiswal, Tilak, Rinku, Abhishek, Suryavanshi among them). Bowlers: 8 of 12. The ones missed were mostly Test picks or wicket-taking quicks.

Board built the day BCCI's final list came out, from every active uncapped Indian (about 800), using only data from before that day. A random 100 would hold 3 or 4. The old board, which only knew players from past auction lists, had 8 in its top 100 for Dec 2025 and could never show a first timer. Some buys can't be scored at all: in Dec 2025, 8 of the 39 had no senior T20 games in our data (picked from state leagues, age group cricket or trials).

Why this October view is early

Built in October 2025, the same board would have held only 12 of the 39 eventual buys, because Prashant Veer, Mukul Choudhary and others earned their deals in the Syed Mushtaq Ali Trophy and state leagues that ran after October. The board gets rebuilt once this season's games are played and again when BCCI's list is out.

The edge

Teams pay for wickets and pace. Wickets don't repeat; economy does. Players tagged Underrated rate well on what repeats but the market tends to pass on them; Likely overrated is the reverse.
